Constituency-level breakdown

The uniform swing from step 1 is applied to each valkrets' actual 2022 result, then seats are allocated within each constituency separately. Each constituency is shaded by whichever party (or bloc) leads on projected vote share there — click a region to see the underlying vote share and seat breakdown. This is where the 12% constituency threshold is checked for parties polling under 4% nationally.

Constituency-level seat counts are an estimate: the real system allocates 310 fixed seats per-constituency with a 1.2 divisor, then separately assigns 39 national levelling seats to specific constituencies through a comparative-figure procedure. This map instead runs one proportional calculation per constituency across its full historical seat total, which reproduces the geographic pattern closely but will not always match the official fixed/levelling split seat-for-seat. The national totals in the seat projection section are the authoritative figures. Constituency boundaries are simplified from municipality-level geodata.
Methodology & assumptions

Baseline data. 2022 vote shares and total seats for all 29 constituencies are taken from the Election Authority's (Valmyndigheten) final results.

Swing model. For each party, swing = (your input) − (2022 national share). That same percentage-point swing is added to the party's 2022 share in every constituency, then each constituency's shares are rescaled to sum to 100%. This is a "uniform national swing" model — it assumes the change in support is geographically even, which is a simplification real elections rarely satisfy exactly, but it is the standard approach used by pollsters and forecasters absent constituency-level polling.

Thresholds. A party qualifies for the national seat pool only if its national vote share is ≥4%. A party under 4% nationally can still win constituency seats in any single valkrets where its swung local vote share is ≥12% — the tool checks this for every constituency and flags it when triggered.

Modified Sainte-Laguë. Seats are awarded one at a time to whichever qualifying party has the highest quotient (votes ÷ divisor). The first divisor is 1.4; after a party wins its first seat, its divisor becomes 3, then 5, 7, 9… (2×seats already won, plus 1).

What this tool does not model: turnout changes, new parties not listed here, regional swing variation, candidate-level personal votes, or the exact fixed/levelling seat split within a constituency (see the note in section 4).
