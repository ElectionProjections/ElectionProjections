Methodology. First preferences and constituency seat counts are the real, official 29 November 2024 result, published by the Houses of the Oireachtas and the Department of Housing, Local Government and Heritage. Moving a party's national slider scales its real local first-preference share in every constituency it contested by the same ratio; a party's slider moving above its 2024 level also lets it compete in constituencies it didn't contest in 2024, using its average share elsewhere as a stand-in, phased in only above baseline. The counting engine runs a genuine STV count in every constituency: Droop quota, surplus transfers on election, elimination transfers on exclusion, and a same-party self-transfer (capped by how many candidates a party's own first-preference strength could plausibly support) so a dominant party can win a justified second seat rather than losing its whole surplus to rivals.

Transfer data. Unlike a hand-estimated matrix, every transfer percentage here is derived directly from the official count-by-count results for all 43 constituencies — tracking each candidate's vote total after every single round, attributing gains and losses by party, and aggregating nationally. Two corrections were applied on top of the raw aggregation: rates are calculated only across constituencies where the receiving party actually stood (otherwise constituencies where a party wasn't on the ballot would dilute its true transfer strength wherever it was), and surplus-distribution transfers are modelled separately from exclusion transfers for the three largest parties, where the real sample size is large enough to support it.
